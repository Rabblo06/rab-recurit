Get started

A venue needs a team for Friday. A manager needs to know who's booked. And staff need

to know where to be, and what happens next.

Crewflow connects that whole journey, from the first request to the final timesheet.

Managers run their agency from the web console. Each manager has a private

workspace, with their own staff, venues and shifts.

Venue managers use the Crewflow app to request staff. Staff use the same app to

see their offers, clock in, and check their hours.

It starts with the team. In the web console, the manager keeps a record for

every staff member and every venue.

Each venue manager is linked to a venue, and the manager decides which staff each

one can work with.

Here, Riley Chen is the venue manager at Riverside House.

In the app, Riley sees that assigned team, and no one else.

On Tuesday, Riley needs four waiting staff for a wedding reception on Friday.

Riley chooses the venue, the role, the time and the break, then picks four people

from the team.

If someone isn't normally available that day, Crewflow warns Riley before anything

is sent.

The request goes to the manager for approval. Nothing reaches the staff yet.

In Venue Offers, the manager checks the request, adds or removes people if needed,

and approves it.

Approval sends each person their own offer, with the venue, the time, and their

estimated pay.

Priya opens hers and accepts. Because this shift came from an approved request, she's

booked straight away.

It's different when the manager creates a shift directly. Then, accepting holds the

place until the manager confirms it.

Either way, the manager can follow every person on the staffing board in Venue

Offers.

On Friday, Priya's confirmed shift is waiting in her app. Clock-in opens shortly

before it starts.

At the venue, she scans the shift's QR code, and Crewflow's server checks it.

Some venues also require her to be on site.

Her shift is now live, timed by Crewflow's own clock, not her phone's.

When the shift ends, she scans the same code again to clock out.

Afterwards, Riley opens the shift report.

Three people clocked in and out. Isla clocked in, but never clocked out, so

Crewflow flags the missing clock-out instead of guessing her hours.

Riley enters the right time, gives a reason, and saves the correction.

Saving a correction doesn't approve it. Riley still marks each person's hours as

reviewed, one by one.

Rating each person's work is a separate, optional step.

Finalise & send only unlocks once everyone is clocked out and reviewed.

Riley confirms the break details, signs off by typing a full name, and sends. That

locks the hours.

Crewflow then creates the final timesheet as a PDF, queues it for delivery, and

shows when it's been sent.

In the app, the finished shift moves to History.

In the web console, the manager can open both the original and the signed

timesheet.

Back in the web console, the hours appear in Payroll, with the pay they add up to.

Crewflow doesn't move any money.

Ratings from venue managers build up on each staff member's record.

And the dashboard and calendar show the week ahead, the hours worked, and how

ready the next shift is.

Behind the scenes, the web console and the app share one Crewflow service. On every

request, it checks who you are, and which workspace you belong to.

Background jobs look after reminders, expiries and documents.

Request. Approve. Work. Review. Sign off. That's Crewflow.
